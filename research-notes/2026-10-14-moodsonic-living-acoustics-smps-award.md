## Editorial guardrails

- This is a supplier-authored company-news item, not an independent study, award jury report or completed-project acoustic assessment. Attribute the system definition, campaign activities and implementation claims to Moodsonic.
- Do **not** turn the article’s phrase “measurable results” into an assertion of a specific result: the source gives no reverberation, speech-privacy, STI, SPL, occupant-survey, soundscape, noise, lighting, glare, energy or post-occupancy figures; nor does it state which methods, rooms or configurations were measured.
- The article treats soundscaping, acoustic treatment and biophilic design as complementary—not interchangeable—layers. For B2B readers, that supports an early, multi-layer briefing process, but it is not evidence that a soundscape system replaces absorption, isolation, HVAC-noise control or a project-specific acoustic design.
- The source contains no acoustic-lighting product or lighting-performance claim. An acoustic-lighting interpretation should therefore focus on cross-discipline coordination: a lighting layout, controls and visual-comfort brief need to be developed alongside the room’s acoustic, soundscape, AV, HVAC, life-safety, ceiling-access and maintenance requirements.

## Suggested factual outline for the entry

Moodsonic reported on October 12 that it and AKRF developed Living Acoustics, a framework which links soundscaping, acoustic treatment and biophilic design. The company said AKRF’s associated campaign received a 2026 SMPS New York Award of Excellence. Moodsonic also said it supported the campaign through AIA architect training, industry events and application at AKRF’s Manhattan office and client projects.

The announcement is a useful signal that workplace sound can be framed across behaviour, materials and sensory design, rather than as a single late-stage finish. It does not, however, publish the award criteria, its underlying evidence, detailed project locations, product lists, room configurations or results. Buyers should keep the distinction between a design framework and verified performance clear.

For an acoustic-lighting buyer, the practical lesson is to establish shared room-by-room objectives before design packages split: activities and occupancy, speech and privacy needs, background noise and HVAC/AV inputs, existing absorptive surfaces, soundscape operating scenarios, illuminance and glare, controls, service access and commissioning. Any acoustic luminaire still requires configuration-specific acoustic evidence and independent lighting data, alongside a whole-room solution for absorption and sound isolation.
