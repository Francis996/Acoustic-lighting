## Confirmed facts for a news entry

1. Edward Clay & Son published this news/case-study article on October 1, 2026. It presents needle-punch acoustic felt as a material for studios, screening rooms, open-plan offices and hospitality/public interiors. [Original article](https://edwardclay.co.uk/2026/10/01/acoustic-felt-studios-cinemas-offices/)
2. The company explains that sound passing through porous, dense fibrous felt dissipates energy, and says this can reduce reverberation, making speech more intelligible and background noise less fatiguing. [Original article](https://edwardclay.co.uk/2026/10/01/acoustic-felt-studios-cinemas-offices/)
3. For open-plan offices, the article identifies hard floors, glass partitions and high occupancy as contributors to noise, and names suspended baffles, wall panels and acoustic screens made with felt wadding as treatment forms. [Original article](https://edwardclay.co.uk/2026/10/01/acoustic-felt-studios-cinemas-offices/)
4. Edward Clay & Son says felt-panel performance depends on density, thickness and fibre-matrix structure. It says thicker, denser felt absorbs lower frequencies more effectively, while thinner panels work at mid and high frequencies. [Original article](https://edwardclay.co.uk/2026/10/01/acoustic-felt-studios-cinemas-offices/)
5. The article describes wool felt as naturally fire-resistant to a degree, moisture-buffering and low in VOCs, and says natural-fibre felt can support material-health and sustainability credits in BREEAM, WELL or similar frameworks. [Original article](https://edwardclay.co.uk/2026/10/01/acoustic-felt-studios-cinemas-offices/)

## Editorial guardrails

- This is first-party supplier educational content, not an independent test report, installed-project study or room-acoustic calculation. Attribute acoustic mechanism, performance, applications and sustainability claims to Edward Clay & Son.
- The article gives no product grade, thickness, density, mounting, coverage, reverberation measurement, absorption coefficient, fire-classification test report, VOC certificate, project size, cost or post-occupancy result. Do not turn the general material discussion into a prediction for a particular workplace.
- The defensible B2B angle is specification discipline for open-plan interiors: define the zone’s use, noise sources, geometry, hard finishes, available treatment area, target criteria and coordination with HVAC, lighting, access and maintenance; then request configuration-specific acoustic and compliance evidence.
- For acoustic-lighting projects, a felt baffle or ceiling treatment and an acoustic luminaire should each carry their own tested acoustic configuration. Lighting photometry, glare, electrical, suspension and controls information remains a separate project requirement.
