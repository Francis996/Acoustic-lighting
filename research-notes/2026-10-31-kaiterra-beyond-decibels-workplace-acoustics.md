## What the source supports

- Kaiterra published **“Beyond Decibels: Why One Number Can't Describe a Building's Acoustics”** in its official learning-resource library on October 29, 2026. The article explains that a decibel reading captures sound energy over a period but does not by itself describe frequency content, variation over time, or why rooms with similar levels can feel different. [Kaiterra resource](https://learn.kaiterra.com/en/resources/beyond-decibels)
- The source frames continuous workplace sound data as a way to identify when a space becomes loud, how it varies and which frequencies dominate, instead of depending on a single quiet-period spot reading. [Kaiterra resource](https://learn.kaiterra.com/en/resources/beyond-decibels)
- In a companion October 29 Kaiterra resource, the publisher says sound sensors designed not to record or retain speech can show how a room sounds without revealing what people said. That is a general privacy-design distinction, not proof that every sensor or deployment has that feature. [Kaiterra, “Can My Employer Hear Me? Workplace Sound Monitoring & Privacy”](https://learn.kaiterra.com/en/resources/workplace-sound-monitoring-privacy)
