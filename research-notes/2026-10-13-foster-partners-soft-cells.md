## Confirmed facts for a news entry

1. Foster + Partners Industrial Design published *Spotlight: Soft Cells* on **October 13, 2026**. The article revisits Soft Cells, an acoustic-panel system first developed more than two decades ago. [Original article](https://www.fosterandpartnersindustrialdesign.com/news/spotlight-soft-cells/)
2. The practice says it developed Soft Cells with Kvadrat Acoustics as a tensioned-fabric acoustic-panel system designed to fit seamlessly into architectural spaces. Its origin was 30 St Mary Axe in London, where glass and stone created a highly reflective acoustic environment. [Original article](https://www.fosterandpartnersindustrialdesign.com/news/spotlight-soft-cells/)
3. Foster + Partners Industrial Design says its material and acoustics research led to a system that combines textile surfaces, lightweight frames and acoustic sublayers; resolved corners maintain fabric tension and flatness. [Original article](https://www.fosterandpartnersindustrialdesign.com/news/spotlight-soft-cells/)
4. The source says the panels can be adapted to three-dimensional and triangular geometries and integrated into ceilings, walls, partitions and structures. [Original article](https://www.fosterandpartnersindustrialdesign.com/news/spotlight-soft-cells/)
5. The article identifies examples including triangular panels in the Samson Pavilion at Cleveland Clinic, acoustic treatment in Al Rasheed Hall at House of Wisdom, and installations at Foster + Partners’ Main Studio and 50 Electric Boulevard in London. [Original article](https://www.fosterandpartnersindustrialdesign.com/news/spotlight-soft-cells/)
6. The practice frames Soft Cells as a move from architecture-informed products toward building-integrated systems; it says the work also raised questions about what could occupy the gaps between panels, contributing to the later development of its Node building-services family. [Original article](https://www.fosterandpartnersindustrialdesign.com/news/spotlight-soft-cells/)

## Editorial guardrails

- This is a first-party design-practice retrospective, not an independent acoustic-test report, a product launch specification sheet or a measured post-occupancy study. Attribute system construction, integration range and project examples to Foster + Partners Industrial Design.
- The article supplies no absorption coefficient, test standard, mounting build-up, panel depth, coverage percentage, room volume, reverberation target/result, fire classification, photometry, lighting-control specification or pricing. Do not assert acoustic performance values or claim that the system will achieve a particular result in a buyer’s room.
- The useful B2B takeaway is systems coordination: acoustic treatment can become a building surface rather than a late applied layer, but its geometry, substructure, services voids, access, fire strategy and maintenance must be developed against the room’s sound criteria.
- For acoustic-lighting buyers, use the piece to discuss early coordination of ceilings/walls with luminaires, controls, HVAC, sprinklers and access panels. An acoustic luminaire still needs its own configuration-specific absorption and photometric evidence; the article does not document an acoustic-lighting product.
