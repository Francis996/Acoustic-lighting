## Confirmed facts for a news entry

1. Hyundai Motor Company and Tate jointly announced the opening of **Hyundai Commission: Tarek Atoui: Hold** on **October 12, 2026**. The announcement describes it as the 11th Hyundai Commission and a major multisensory installation by artist and composer Tarek Atoui. [Original announcement](https://org.hyundai.com/worldwide/en/newsroom/detail/0000001286)
2. The release says Atoui transformed Tate Modern’s Turbine Hall into an immersive sonic landscape that combines custom-built wind instruments, percussion, electronics and sculptural objects. It says participation and performance will allow the environment to evolve during the commission. [Original announcement](https://org.hyundai.com/worldwide/en/newsroom/detail/0000001286)
3. According to the release, the artist responded to the Turbine Hall’s height, airflows and acoustic character. It identifies a metal karnai, two 3D-printed horns, drum skins, gongs, horns, stones, 20-metre textiles, bamboo and shengs, with wind channelled by a computer-controlled motor. [Original announcement](https://org.hyundai.com/worldwide/en/newsroom/detail/0000001286)
4. The source says embedded microphones sample instrument and Turbine Hall sounds, while custom software reworks and plays them back as layers of the composition. It also says live sound-based performances will take place during the six-month commission. [Original announcement](https://org.hyundai.com/worldwide/en/newsroom/detail/0000001286)
5. The announcement lists the public run as **October 13, 2026–April 11, 2027** at Tate Modern, in partnership with Hyundai Motor. [Original announcement](https://org.hyundai.com/worldwide/en/newsroom/detail/0000001286)

## Suggested factual outline for the entry

Hyundai Motor Company and Tate announced the opening of *Hyundai Commission: Tarek Atoui: Hold* on October 12, 2026. The joint release presents the 11th Hyundai Commission as a multisensory installation by artist and composer Tarek Atoui, running at Tate Modern from October 13, 2026 to April 11, 2027. It says the Turbine Hall installation combines custom-built wind instruments, percussion, electronics and sculptural objects into an evolving sonic environment.

The source says the work responds to the hall’s height, airflows and acoustic character. It describes instruments, textiles, bamboo, stones and computer-controlled wind, alongside microphones and custom software that sample and rework sounds from both the instruments and the busy hall. The programme also includes live sound-based performances during the commission.

For FLOSEEK readers, the useful lesson is experiential rather than a product specification: sound can be treated as a material and behavioural layer alongside architecture, light, circulation and participation. Commercial buyers should still set measurable acoustic and visual objectives for each room and obtain configuration- and project-specific technical evidence; this announcement does not establish a transferable acoustic or lighting-performance result.
