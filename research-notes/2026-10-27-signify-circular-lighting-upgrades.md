## What the source supports

- Signify says the University of Oxford’s Circular Economy and Sustainability Lab and Academics for Circular Economy e.V., with Signify support, analyzed 50 real-world lighting projects that used circular approaches. [Signify release](https://www.signify.com/global/our-company/news/press-releases/2026/20261026-oxford-analysis-links-circular-approaches-to-business-benefits-in-74-percent-of-real-world-lighting-projects-studied)
- The source states that 37 projects reported capital, maintenance or financing benefits. It says the sample covered 10 sectors, including hospitals, schools, airports, offices, ports, tunnels and municipal infrastructure. [Signify release](https://www.signify.com/global/our-company/news/press-releases/2026/20261026-oxford-analysis-links-circular-approaches-to-business-benefits-in-74-percent-of-real-world-lighting-projects-studied)
- Across the 50 projects, the release reports extended asset life in 31, asset or material retention in 26, avoided disruption in 23 and avoided full replacement in 20. It says 36 projects reported energy savings and gives a 59.5% median reduction. [Signify release](https://www.signify.com/global/our-company/news/press-releases/2026/20261026-oxford-analysis-links-circular-approaches-to-business-benefits-in-74-percent-of-real-world-lighting-projects-studied)
- Signify cites a Dublin Port Tunnel upgrade of about 1,800 light points and estimates €3 million in capital savings against a completely new LED system. [Signify release](https://www.signify.com/global/our-company/news/press-releases/2026/20261026-oxford-analysis-links-circular-approaches-to-business-benefits-in-74-percent-of-real-world-lighting-projects-studied)
