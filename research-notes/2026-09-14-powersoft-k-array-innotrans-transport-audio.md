## Confirmed facts for the news entry

1. Powersoft and K-array say they will share Stand 870, Hall 4.1 at InnoTrans 2026 in Berlin, which runs September 22–25, with each brand showing transport-sector solutions.
2. Powersoft says it will exhibit ENEA, a PAVA platform certified to EN54-16. The announcement says the platform combines background music and everyday announcements with certified emergency voice-alarm messages.
3. The company says ENEA works with its AnyMATE technology, which remotely monitors each loudspeaker and signal path so faults can be identified without on-site checks. It positions the pairing for stations and transport hubs where installation and maintenance simplicity matter.
4. K-array describes WALL as wall-mounted line-array loudspeakers for stations and concourses with wide, controlled horizontal coverage; POLE as IP66-rated pole-mounted loudspeakers for exposed areas such as platforms; and HORN for long-distance, high-power voice reproduction in acoustically demanding environments such as tunnels.
5. The announcement describes Prisma as a digital matrix that combines DSP, audio routing and amplification with Dante networking for centralised operator control. It also says the stand will demonstrate the POLE822TG loudspeaker integrated with AnyMATE.

## Editorial guardrails

- This is a **manufacturer event announcement and product-positioning statement**, not an independent measurement study, completed transport project case study, code analysis, or confirmation that a whole station meets a specified life-safety or speech-intelligibility requirement.
- Attribute product and suitability claims to Powersoft or K-array. Do not add unauthorised claims about STI/STIPA, SPL, coverage maps, evacuation time, reliability, cybersecurity, interoperability, pricing, availability, approvals outside the stated EN54-16 certification, or installed performance.
- The entry can connect the announcement to a transport-hub coordination brief: emergency and routine announcements, background sound, acoustic conditions, loudspeaker layout, ceiling and wall finishes, passenger flows, visual and lighting coordination, network infrastructure, maintenance access, and authority requirements must be resolved project by project.
- Acoustic lighting should be framed only as an adjacent architectural coordination topic. It does not substantiate public-address, voice-alarm, acoustic-treatment, or regulatory performance on its own.
