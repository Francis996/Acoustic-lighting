## Verified facts suitable for copy

1. The article defines speech privacy as preventing conversations from being clearly understood outside their intended area; people may still recognise that someone is speaking. [Acoustical Surfaces](https://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/blog/acoustics-education/speech-privacy-office-design/)
2. It distinguishes speech privacy (the outcome) from sound isolation, sound absorption and sound masking (tools that may contribute). [Acoustical Surfaces](https://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/blog/acoustics-education/speech-privacy-office-design/)
3. The company says sound isolation limits speech transmission through walls, doors, ceilings, floors and other building assemblies, while absorption reduces reflections within a room but does not stop conversations travelling to adjacent spaces. [Acoustical Surfaces](https://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/blog/acoustics-education/speech-privacy-office-design/)
4. It says sound masking can improve privacy in open workspaces, but cannot replace the isolation needed for confidential conversations. [Acoustical Surfaces](https://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/blog/acoustics-education/speech-privacy-office-design/)
5. The post identifies flanking paths including door undercuts, gaps around frames, shared ceiling plenums, ductwork, pipe penetrations, recessed fixtures and incomplete partitions. [Acoustical Surfaces](https://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/blog/acoustics-education/speech-privacy-office-design/)
6. It recommends coordinating walls, ceilings, doors, windows and HVAC; it also says lighting, HVAC, sprinklers, speakers and access panels can create or affect speech-transmission paths if not coordinated. [Acoustical Surfaces](https://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/blog/acoustics-education/speech-privacy-office-design/)
7. The company says open offices cannot provide true speech privacy by themselves because they share the same air space; enclosed rooms are needed where conversations must remain confidential. [Acoustical Surfaces](https://www.acousticalsurfaces.com/blog/acoustics-education/speech-privacy-office-design/)

## Copy guardrails

- Attribute the framework and all recommendations to Acoustical Surfaces.
- Do not present the post as third-party performance research, a building-code requirement, a completed-project result or a comparative product test.
- Do not infer absorption coefficients, STC values, speech-privacy ratings, room-level reverberation results, lighting calculations or a specific acoustic-lighting product claim.
- The FLOSEEK interpretation can make a bounded specification point: coordinate the ceiling plane early, because luminaire cut-outs, ceiling interfaces and services may compromise a complete privacy strategy. Acoustic luminaires can improve absorption and lighting in the appropriate zone but do not provide isolation on their own.
